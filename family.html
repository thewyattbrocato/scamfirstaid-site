Help a family member without taking over

Shame is the scammer's best friend. It keeps people quiet. This playbook gives you words that keep trust while you protect each other.

Tailor your playbook

Your family playbook

This starter version works with JavaScript turned off. Answer the two questions above to tailor it. Then print it and keep it by the phone.

Open the conversation

"Mom, Dad - you have handled harder things than this. A scam is going around that fooled a lot of sharp people. Can we compare notes for five minutes?"

You are a second pair of eyes, not an auditor. These words keep trust while you talk about scams.

Take the shame off the table

  • "Scammers are professionals at what they do. This happens to smart people every day."
  • "These crews run call centers and work from scripts. Anyone can be caught off guard on a busy day."

Offer help, not a takeover

  • "Would you like help sorting anything? It is your call. I can just be a second pair of eyes on mail and messages if that is useful."
  • "If you ever get a weird request for money, hang up. Call me before you decide. No question is too small."
  • "Thank you for talking with me about this. Let's do the safe word now so it is settled."

Words for this moment

They are talking to the scammer right now

  • "Are you safe? Good. Hang up on them now."
  • "Stay on the line with me. Call your bank back on the number on your card."
  • "Do not buy anything. Do not mail anything. Do not read out any codes."
  • "We will sort the rest out together. One step at a time."

Money or information is already gone

  • "Thank you for telling me. That took guts."
  • "Scammers are professionals. This happens to smart people every day."
  • "Let us write down what happened while it is fresh."
  • "I will sit with you while you make the calls."

They do not believe it is a scam

  • "You do not have to agree with me today."
  • "Would you do one small thing for me?"
  • "Let me read the messages with you. Two sets of eyes beat one."
  • "You are sharp. If I am wrong, I will say so."

Close calls keep happening

  • "Let us practice the hang-up once, for fun."
  • "When a call feels urgent, hang up first."
  • "Then call back on a number you already know."
  • "Soon your hands will know what to do."

Money habits are starting to change

  • "I am not worried about you. I am worried about the people who target our age group."
  • "Can we set up bank alerts together, so we both relax?"
  • "It takes five minutes. You stay in charge."

Safe-word protocol starter

Crooks can copy a voice from a short clip posted online. A spoken safe word beats a familiar voice.

  1. Pick a word no one would guess from your family's public life. Avoid pet names and birthdays.
  2. Agree on the rule. Any urgent request for money or details gets a hang-up first, then a call back on a known number.
  3. Ask for the safe word before acting on any urgent plea.
  4. Practice once now, for fun, so using it later feels normal.

Print and sign your family agreement

  • Our family safe word is: ______________________________
  • If an urgent request mentions money or personal details, we hang up first.
  • We call back on a number we already know, never one the caller gives us.
  • We ask for the safe word before we act.
  • No shame either way. We would all rather double-check.

Change the safe word once a year. Put it on your birthday card list.

Practice the safe word in two minutes

You play the scammer

  • "Ring ring. This is your bank. There is a problem. Read me the code on your phone."
  • "No safe word? Then I hang up. Goodbye."
  • "You did it right. That was perfect."

They practice the defense

  • "Hold on. What is our safe word?"
  • "No word, no deal. I hang up and call my bank myself."
  • "That was fun. I feel ready now."

When they push back

  • Make it normal. "This crew fooled an engineer I know. They fool smart people for a living."
  • Shrink the ask. "Just one statement, together, once. Five minutes over coffee."
  • Hand back control. "You decide. I only drive if you ask me to."

If money keeps leaving, I will call for outside help. I will do it because I love you, even if you are angry with me.

A letter you can mail or hand over

Tone lands better on paper than in a rushed call. Fill the blanks. Send it as is.

Dear ____,

I read that scammers tricked a lot of careful people this year. You taught me to double-check things. I use that lesson every day.

If anything odd ever reaches you, a call, letter, or message about money, I would love to be your second pair of eyes. You decide what to share. I promise no lectures.

If you ever want, we can pick a family code word together. It takes two minutes.

Love, ____

Monitoring ladder

  1. Alerts on. Turn on text or email alerts for bank and card activity. This is free in every banking app.
  2. Statements together. Once a month, look over statements together over coffee. Make it routine, not an audit.
  3. View-only access. Ask the bank about view-only access or a trusted-contact form. Your family member stays in charge.
  4. Legal-caregiver guides. Read the free guides for family money helpers. They list the rules in plain words. CFPB: Managing Someone Else's Money.
  5. Outside help. Money still leaving, or contact will not stop? Ask Adult Protective Services for help. Call the Eldercare Locator to reach them. 1-800-677-1116, eldercare.acl.gov.

When to bring in more help

  • Still in contact with scammers, or money keeps leaving? Call Adult Protective Services through the Eldercare Locator: 1-800-677-1116, eldercare.acl.gov.
  • Age 60 or older and scammed? The DOJ National Elder Fraud Hotline walks you through reporting: 1-833-372-8311 (Mon-Fri, 10 a.m.-6 p.m. ET), official page.
  • Feeling hopeless or overwhelmed? The 988 Lifeline offers free, private support day and night. Call or text 988.
  • Anyone in immediate danger? Call 911.
  • Papers or legal forms in question? Talk to an elder-law attorney. Your local Area Agency on Aging can point you to one. Find yours through the Eldercare Locator: 1-800-677-1116, eldercare.acl.gov.
